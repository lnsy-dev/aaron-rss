Aaron RSS — Help

Aaron RSS is a simple, local-first RSS reader for the desktop. It keeps your feeds and reading state in a private SQLite database stored on your computer, and it can fetch fresh articles automatically in the background.

What you can do

  • Subscribe to RSS, Atom, and JSON feeds by URL.
  • Read articles in a clean in-app viewer.
  • Open the original website directly when a feed or article calls for it.
  • Mark articles read/unread and star the ones you want to keep.
  • Search inside articles with the find bar.
  • Export and import your subscription list as OPML.
  • Automatically download YouTube videos from feeds that have the option enabled.

Feeds

Each feed is shown as a collapsible block. The number next to the feed title is the count of unread articles. Click the feed title to expand or collapse it.

Click the ⋯ kebab menu on a feed to:

  • Mark all articles read or unread
  • Refresh just that feed
  • Toggle Open Original by Default
  • Toggle Auto Download YouTube Videos
  • Visit the feed's website
  • Delete the feed
Tip: "Open Original by Default" makes every article in that feed open the publisher's website instead of the extracted reading view.

YouTube videos

Articles that link to YouTube videos are marked with a ▶ YouTube badge. Clicking them opens an embedded player.

  • A Delete Video button is visible while the video plays.
  • When the video ends, choose Save to keep the article in your feed or Delete to remove the downloaded file and mark the article read.
  • Feeds with Auto Download YouTube Videos enabled will download YouTube videos in the background during refresh.
  • YouTube live streams and gaming.youtube.com links are ignored.

Settings

Open Settings from the command panel or hamburger menu to adjust:

  • Max articles per feed — how many unread articles to show at once
  • Auto-refresh interval — how often feeds refresh automatically (set to 0 to disable)
  • Show unread articles only — hide read articles from the list
  • Custom theme CSS — personalize colors and typography

Data and privacy

Your feeds, articles, read state, and settings are stored locally in a SQLite database using the browser's Origin Private File System (OPFS). Nothing is sent to a central server. You can export your subscriptions as OPML from the command panel to back them up or move them to another reader.

Note: If you clear site data for this app, your local database will be removed. Use Export OPML to keep your subscription list safe.
